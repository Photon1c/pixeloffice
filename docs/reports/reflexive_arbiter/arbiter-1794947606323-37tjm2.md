## Scope

- **System:** CriticalInfrastructure-Nov2026
- **Description:** Comparative governance divergence across five critical-infrastructure institutions experiencing November 2026 incidents (Microsoft Outlook Nov 12; FAA Nov 13 ground stop; United Airlines Nov 16; Google Nov 16 autonomous cyberattack findings; NSA Nov 17 internal communications outage). Focus is recurrent patterns in maintenance posture, dependency disclosure, recovery narrative, and accountability framing.
- **Institutional Context:** Cross-institutional comparative review of five U.S. federal/critical-infrastructure incidents occurring between Nov 12–17, 2026. The investigative premise is that increasingly institutions are reducing real-world activity (grounding aircraft, staggering arrivals) to accommodate limits in their digital coordination infrastructure. Outcomes must distinguish documented facts, causal hypotheses, and extrapolated risks, and must not presume coordinated attacks, shared technical dependencies, or AI involvement without supporting evidence.
- **Boundaries:** Configuration-management and maintenance practices across five institutions, Institutional press releases, public statements, and self-reported post-event reports, Independent press (Reuters, AP, Wired) and court-of-record sources (GAO, IG reports), Industry post-mortems and vendor-published operational reports, Comparative telemetry where institutions disclose time-to-restore or time-to-validate metrics

## Evidence-Backed Dependency Analysis

The five Nov 2026 incidents reveal a small set of recurring dependency shapes. None of them meet a "shared technical-dependency" criterion — each incident identifies different underlying components — but the *shape* of the dependency disclosure and the *shape* of the recovery posture are recognisably the same. This section enumerates those shapes with provenance.

1. **N-of-1 maintenance configuration authority.** NSA's Nov 17 statement attributes its outage to a configuration error inside one scheduled maintenance window (verified; NSA_PRIMARY_URI). The same "configuration error" framing recurs in U.S. federal IT post-event reports (inferred; llm_assertion). FAA's NOTAM Nov 13 outage showed a similar narrow blast radius for what is functionally a national-scope system.
2. **Single visible-to-user state as the only gate.** In every incident, the visibility surface is binary: work vs. does not work. There is no published third-state (degraded, partial, queued) in any of the five public releases. Microsoft Outlook's status page is the single exception that publishes a third state — and that surfacing is itself a positive outlier.
3. **Primary-path return rather than parallel capability.** No institution in the dataset references backup-system exercise or fail-over cadence in their public statement. FAA's mean recovery is documented internally; the others do not commit to a recovery-time metric in their statement.
4. **Public relations as the published accountability surface.** Each institution's primary statement is signed by a public-affairs function (NSA Office of Public Affairs; airline media desk; vendor customer-comms). Technical or executive accountability is not named in the public statement itself; in the GAO pattern, accountability is documented months later in a separate report.

**What this does *not* show.** No shared technical dependency across institutions has been proven. No coordinated attack vector has been identified. No AI involvement is reported in any of the five incidents. These are coordinated-shaped, but not coordinated-evidenced, failures; pattern evidence alone is not sufficient for the coordinated-attack hypothesis at any reasonable confidence level.

## Recovery Capacity and Institutional Saturation Indicators

Saturation here is operational, not technical — recovery throughput is bounded by the institution's ability to reduce or stage world activity, not by machine capacity. Five indicators carry the signal:

## Longitudinal Monitoring Recommendations

2. **Operational backlog-clearance time versus technical-restoration time.** Adopt the telemetry shape (this run uses hours) as a standing measurement. 4. **Gate-and-stagger operation tally.** Count the number of ground-stops, voluntary capacity reductions, and staggered-arrival directives issued per quarter across federal/critical-infrastructure institutions. 5. **External-press-as-visibility ratio.** Of a stratified sample of incidents, the share in which the only public blast-radius disclosure came from independent journalism. A rising share indicates institutional visibility is failing in the role.
6. **Named-accountability disclosure rate.** Of incidents in the corpus, the share that names an individual or function as the accountable party in the public statement. Microsoft Outlook (Nov 12) is the dataset outlier on this metric; tracking whether other institutions converge is the longitudinal accountability signal.
7. **Cannot-tolerate-defund function roster.** Track per-institution the named functions whose competency cohort is documented as <5 individuals (mainframe engineers, ensemble behaviour owners, cleared-systems administrators).
