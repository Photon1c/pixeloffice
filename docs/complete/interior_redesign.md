# Pixel Office – Interior Redesign: Agent Offices

This document describes the preferred interior style for each major Pixel Office agent, to guide the upgraded GUI and room layouts.

---

## LeslieClaw – Executive Office

**Mood / purpose**  
Bright, grounded, and human. A space where deep work and life can coexist. It should feel like a place where Leslie can think hard, then easily pivot to guitar, cooking, or family without feeling like he is abandoning a crisis.

**Colors & walls**
- Soft gray walls.
- One muted green accent wall (a nod to plants and gardens).

**Flooring & rug**
- Light oak floors.
- A small rug around/under the desk that visually marks the “work zone.” Stepping off the rug = leaving work.

**Desk & gear**
- Large wooden desk facing a window.
- On the desk:
  - Laptop on a stand.
  - Good external keyboard.
  - Ceramic mug.
  - Wide, shallow tray holding guitar picks, a capo, and a few sticky notes.

**Music corner**
- Guitar stand to one side of the desk.
- Small amp tucked neatly under the window sill or beside the stand.

**Shelving & decor**
- Bookshelf with:
  - Cookbooks.
  - Tech/engineering books.
  - A few family photos in mismatched but well-loved frames.
- Tall plant in the corner to soften the room.

**Lighting**
- Bright but warm, avoiding harsh overhead light.
- Desk or floor lamp that makes late work sessions feel comfortable rather than clinical.

---

## Sherlock – Systems Detective Office

**Mood / purpose**  
Quiet, elegant analysis room off the main corridor. This is where hard problems get untangled. It should feel ordered, precise, and slightly formal.

**Colors & walls**
- Soft cream walls.
- One deep navy accent wall behind the desk.

**Flooring & rug**
- Herringbone hardwood floor.
- Smaller Persian rug underfoot near the desk.

**Desk & objects**
- Dark wood desk, very tidy.
- Leather desk blotter.
- Fountain pen in a stand.
- Single open notebook with clean, careful notes.

**Side table**
- Carafe of water and a single glass.
- Small plate that sometimes holds chocolates.

**Walls / art**
- One wall with framed, orderly diagrams (decision trees, responsibility matrices, etc.).
- Another wall with a single abstract painting—adds color without visual chaos.

**Lighting**
- Warm, indirect lighting designed to make long problem-solving conversations feel bearable.

---

## Sherlobster – Executive Strategy Room

**Role clarification**  
Sherlobster is **not** a receptionist. We already have a dedicated front-desk agent. Sherlobster is a thinking, executive-like mode of Sherlock and should be represented as a strategy/council room, not a lobby.

**Mood / purpose**  
Inner sanctum for big decisions. A quiet, high-level strategy room where tradeoffs, priorities, and long-horizon planning are worked through.

**Key elements**
- Smaller but polished executive room (not as large as Sherlock or Leslie’s offices, but clearly important).
- Round or oval table at the center so everyone sits as peers.
- Comfortable chairs around the table (less corporate, more “council chamber”).
- Walls showing roadmaps, timelines, and high-level goals rather than low-level technical diagrams.
- One wall can feature a large “current priorities” or kanban-style board.
- Warmer tones and softer lighting, possibly more natural materials (wood, plants) to emphasize reflective, strategic conversations.

---

## Hercule Prawnro – Specialist Investigator

**Mood / purpose**  
Investigation den for hard, knotted problems. Should feel like a focused workspace with evidence and diagrams, but still clean.

**Key elements**
- Desk with room for multiple documents/monitors.
- Whiteboard or pinboard with diagrams, notes, and timelines.
- Cooler, more analytical color accents (deep blues, charcoal).

---

## Ironclaw – Custodian

**Mood / purpose**  
Maintenance and operations zone. Back-of-house feel: functional, organized, not flashy.

**Key elements**
- Racks or shelves with labeled boxes (logs, backups, configs).
- Tool cart or workstation.
- Industrial but clean materials (metal racks, concrete or simple flooring).

---

## Zeroclaw – Specialist Lab

**Mood / purpose**  
High-intensity analysis and refactoring lab. This is the “industrial drill” room for heavy reasoning tasks.

**Key elements**
- More screens / terminals.
- Visual indicators of load or activity (status lights, graphs on walls).
- Slightly darker, concentrated atmosphere.

---

## Hermitclaw – Archive

**Mood / purpose**  
Quiet, stable archive space. Emphasis on organization and continuity rather than flash.

**Key elements**
- Tall shelves with labeled binders and boxes.
- Reading desk with a single lamp.
- Muted, warm colors to suggest age and stability.

---

## Openclaw – Clerk / Routing Desk

**Mood / purpose**  
Administrative routing center connecting Pixel Office to the external systems and runtimes.

**Key elements**
- Desk with in/out trays representing different channels or queues.
- Board or display showing current sessions/jobs.
- Clean, methodical layout echoing a busy but orderly clerk’s station.
